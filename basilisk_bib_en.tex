\section*{C. Case law}
\begin{BibList}
\BibItem{SN 61/03}{sn6103} Resolution of seven judges of the Supreme Court (SN) of 6 November 2003, III CZP 61/03, OSNC 2004, No.~5, item 69 (legal principle) --- a contractual penalty is due even if the creditor has suffered no loss; the debtor may request its reduction (Article~484(2) of the Civil Code).
\BibItem{SN 304/00}{sn30400} Judgment of the Supreme Court of 3 October 2000, I CKN 304/00, OSNC 2001, No.~4, item 59 --- information is a trade secret only if the business has taken steps to keep it confidential; disclosure to a narrow circle of persons bound by confidentiality does not deprive it of protection.
\BibItem{SN 120/07}{sn12007} Judgment of the Supreme Court of 8 January 2008, II PK 120/07 --- an employee's liability for damage is exhaustively governed by the Labour Code; a contractual penalty (Article~483 of the Civil Code) stipulated in an employment relationship cannot be enforced.
\BibItem{SN 42/18}{sn4218} Resolution of the Supreme Court of 7 September 2018, III CZP 42/18 --- concerns the representation of a company in agreements with a management board member by an attorney appointed by a shareholders' resolution (Article~210(1) KSH); relevant to the interpretation of Article~300$^{79}$ KSH.
\end{BibList}
