\section*{C. Case law}
\begin{BibList}
\BibItem{SN 61/03}{sn6103} Resolution of seven judges of the Supreme Court (SN) of 6 November 2003, III CZP 61/03, OSNC 2004, No.~5, item 69 (legal principle) --- a contractual penalty is due even if the creditor has suffered no loss; the debtor may request its reduction (Article~484(2) of the Civil Code).
\BibItem{SN 304/00}{sn30400} Judgment of the Supreme Court of 3 October 2000, I CKN 304/00, OSNC 2001, No.~4, item 59 --- an employee's use of information in respect of which the business did not take the necessary steps to keep it confidential is to be treated as use of general knowledge, to which the business has no statutory rights.
\BibItem{SN 120/07}{sn12007} Judgment of the Supreme Court of 8 January 2008, II PK 120/07 --- an employee's liability for damage is exhaustively governed by the Labour Code; a contractual penalty (Article~483 of the Civil Code) stipulated in an employment relationship cannot be enforced; it is, however, permissible in a non-compete agreement after the employment ends (the Court refers to the judgment of 10 October 2003, I PK 528/02, OSNP 2004, No.~19, item 336).
\BibItem{SN 42/18}{sn4218} Resolution of the Supreme Court of 7 September 2018, III CZP 42/18, OSNC 2019, No.~6, item 64 --- Article~210(1) KSH applies to a limited liability company's consent to an amendment of the agreement of a limited partnership in which a management board member is also a partner; the provision covers all acts connected with an agreement between the company and a board member (conclusion, amendment, termination, set-off, settlement), and a potential conflict of interest is sufficient. Relevant to the interpretation of Article~300$^{79}$ KSH.
\BibItem{SA 485/18}{sa48518} Judgment of the Court of Appeal in Warsaw of 9 April 2019, V ACa 485/18 --- a management board member may not, as an attorney, represent a limited liability company in an agreement with another board member (Article~210(1) KSH; the court cites legal writing and Supreme Court resolutions III CZP 71/18 and III CZP 42/18); a breach results in absolute invalidity (Article~58(1) of the Civil Code).
\end{BibList}
