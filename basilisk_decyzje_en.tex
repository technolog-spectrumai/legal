\section{How to use this manual}

\emph{This is an English convenience translation. In the event of any discrepancy, the Polish version prevails.}

This manual summarises the decision-making rules arising from the articles of association (the ``Articles'', version 3.3-S), the shareholders' agreement (the ``SHA'', version 1.7) and the internal documents: the information security policy, the Price List and the NDA template. In this manual, the ``GM'' means the General Meeting, the ``Board'' means the Board of Directors, an ``executive director'' is a director to whom the Board has delegated the day-to-day management of the Company, and a ``non-executive director'' is any other director. References in brackets indicate the source of each rule.

\begin{Uwaga}
This manual does not amend any agreement. In the event of a discrepancy, the Articles prevail, followed by the SHA. Internal limits bind the directors towards the Company but do not affect the validity of agreements with third parties (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:reprezentacja}(4)): a director who exceeds them is liable to the Company.
\end{Uwaga}

Source materials: the Articles \Zr{us}, the SHA \Zr{ua}, the IP agreements \Zr{ip}, the Price List \Zr{cennik}, the NDA \Zr{nda}, the policy \Zr{polityka}; legal bases and literature --- in the bibliography at the end. Section numbers are clickable and open the relevant agreement (the PDF files must be in the same directory).

Colours in the diagrams: \textcolor{cWZ}{\textbf{blue}} --- shareholders and the General Meeting; \textcolor{cRD}{\textbf{green}} --- Board of Directors; \textcolor{cDW}{\textbf{gold}} --- executive director; \textcolor{cX}{\textbf{red}} --- special situations (dispute, court, conflict of interest).

\section{Corporate bodies of the Company}

The Company operates under a one-tier (monistic) system: it has only the General Meeting and the Board of Directors; it has no management board or supervisory board (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:rada}(1)).

\end{tikzpicture}
\caption{Structure of the corporate bodies (Art.~\S\S~\ref{en-art:rada}--\ref{en-art:wz}).}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{Board of Directors} manages the Company's affairs, represents it and supervises it (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:rada}(2)). Resolutions are adopted by an absolute majority of votes with at least half of the directors participating; in the event of a tie, the Chair decides, unless the matter concerns the Chair's own interest (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:rada}(5)).
  \item The \textbf{executive director} runs the day-to-day business under a delegation from the Board. The delegation does not cover matters requiring a Board resolution (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:rada}(4) and (7)).
  \item \textbf{Non-executive director} --- there must be at least one. Represents the Company in agreements and disputes with an executive director on the basis of a resolution of the non-executive directors (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:reprezentacja}(3)).
  \item The \textbf{Rules of the Board of Directors} (adopted by the Board) set out the detailed allocation of powers, spending limits and reporting rules (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:rada}(8)).
\end{itemize}

% ================================================================
\begin{Komentarz}
The one-tier system (Article~300$^{73}$ KSH \Zr{ksh}) shortens the decision path: the same body manages the affairs, represents the Company and supervises. The price is the risk that supervision becomes a fiction if the non-executive director depends on the executive ones. In practice: grant the delegation to the executive director by a resolution stating its date and scope, and have the executive director report to the Board monthly (budget execution, agreements above the thresholds, incidents). More on the PSA model: \Zr{opalski}, \Zr{malinowski}, \Zr{ecfr}.
\end{Komentarz}

\begin{Zasada}
\textbf{``75\% of all votes in the Company''} is calculated on all existing votes, not on those present. An absent or abstaining shareholder therefore acts as a vote ``against''. An \textbf{``absolute majority of votes cast''} means more votes ``for'' than the combined votes ``against'' and ``abstaining'' among the votes cast (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:wz}(7)).
\end{Zasada}

\begin{Komentarz}
The most common mistake is confusing ``75\% of all votes in the Company'' with 75\% of those present. With a 50/20/10/10/10 split, Founder~A alone cannot carry a 75\% resolution but can block any such resolution; A and~B together hold 70\% --- a third Founder is still needed. The consequences of omitting a consent differ: an act performed without a resolution required by \emph{statute} is invalid (Article~17 \S~1 KSH \Zr{ksh}), whereas one performed without a consent required \emph{only by the Articles} is valid towards the counterparty, but the director is liable to the Company (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:reprezentacja}(4)).
\end{Komentarz}

Regardless of the amount: a transaction with a shareholder, director or related entity requires a Board resolution (without the participation of a director having a conflict of interest), and a credit or loan agreement with a director also requires the consent of the GM by 75\% (table~1 item~j). \begin{Komentarz}
The thresholds also apply to related acts: splitting one agreement into parts to stay below a threshold is a circumvention and does not protect the director from liability. The more precisely the Board adopts the budget, the fewer ad hoc resolutions are needed --- the budget is the main tool for delegating decisions to the executive director. The thresholds are not indexed; it is worth reviewing them every 2--3 years.
\end{Komentarz}

\section{How to adopt a resolution}

\subsection*{Shareholders' resolution outside the General Meeting (e-mail)}

The default procedure for most matters. Exception: resolutions requiring notarial minutes (in particular an amendment of the Articles) are adopted at a General Meeting (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:wz}(1)).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=6mm and 5mm]
  \node[wz=2.9cm] (s1) {\textbf{1. Draft}\\the Board, the Chair or a shareholder with $\ge$5\% of votes circulates a draft with reasons};
  \node[wz=2.9cm, right=of s1] (s2) {\textbf{2. Voting}\\by e-mail from the address in the register; deadline: 3 business days -- 21 days};
  \node[wz=2.9cm, right=of s2] (s3) {\textbf{3. Adoption}\\when the deadline expires, or earlier once a majority of all votes is reached};
  \node[wz=2.9cm, right=of s3] (s4) {\textbf{4. Result}\\the Chair determines the result and notifies by e-mail; entry in the minute book};
  \draw[line] (s1) -- (s2); \draw[line] (s2) -- (s3); \draw[line] (s3) -- (s4);
\end{tikzpicture}
\caption{Resolution outside the General Meeting (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:wz}(2)--(3) and (8)).}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A deadline shorter than 3 business days is possible with the consent of all shareholders. A vote cast after the deadline does not count.
  \item Voting \emph{in writing} (on paper) requires the consent of all shareholders to that procedure; voting by e-mail does not require such consent.
  \item A General Meeting without a formal convening is possible if all shares are represented and no one objects (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:wz}(4)). Remote participation is governed by the GM Rules.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Resolution of the Board of Directors}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Chair (or a director) circulates the draft; voting may take place at a meeting, in writing or by e-mail, provided the participants can be identified and the votes recorded (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:rada}(6)).
  \item \textbf{Conflict of interest:} a director whose interest conflicts with that of the Company discloses this and does not vote; such a director is not counted towards the quorum.
  \item Quorum: at least half of the appointed directors. Majority: absolute. In the event of a tie --- the Chair's vote.
  \item The resolution is recorded and kept in the Company's records.
\end{enumerate}

% ================================================================
\begin{Komentarz}
An e-mail sent from the address entered in the share register satisfies the documentary form requirement (Article~77$^{2}$ of the Civil Code (KC) \Zr{kc}). Keep the draft, the votes and the notice of the result in a single thread, and enter the resolution in the minute book (Article~300$^{100}$ KSH). Send the notice of the result immediately --- it provides certainty as to when the resolution was adopted, which matters, among other things, for the deadlines to challenge it.
\end{Komentarz}

\section{Consent to a transfer of shares}

Every transfer or encumbrance of shares requires the consent of the Company, which is granted by the Board of Directors (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:zbywanie}). The Company \textbf{must} grant consent unless one of the five grounds for refusal applies.

\textbf{Grounds for refusal (closed list, Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:zbywanie}(2)):} a)~sanctions or a conflict with the rules on investment control, export control or national security; b)~lack of a required authority approval (conditional consent may be given); c)~lock-up without GM consent; d)~the shares are subject to the transfer obligation and the buyer has not assumed the obligations (power of attorney and offer); e)~the buyer is a competitor (except portfolio investments of up to 5\%).

No consent is required for an acquisition of shares in performance of the transfer obligation or for a transfer in enforcement proceedings (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:zbywanie}(5)).

% ================================================================
\begin{Komentarz}
Consent to a transfer is a non-discretionary decision: the Board does not assess the price or the person of the buyer beyond the list in Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:zbywanie}(2), and a refusal without stating its ground is not an effective refusal. Grounds a--b require screening the buyer against sanctions lists \Zr{sankcje}, \Zr{usank} and for investment control \Zr{uki}, \Zr{fdi} --- it is worth describing the result of the screening in the resolution. If the Company refuses consent without grounds, a final court judgment replaces its declaration (Article~64 of the Civil Code (KC) \Zr{kc}, Article~1047 of the Code of Civil Procedure (KPC) \Zr{kpc}).
\end{Komentarz}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The release on death, incapacity and a Justified Departure takes place \textbf{automatically} under the Articles --- no vote is required (SHA~\S~\ref{shaen:ustanie}(4)).
  \item The transfer agreement on behalf of the departing person may be concluded by an attorney (the Company or a non-executive director) under an irrevocable power of attorney; the buyer pays within 14 days, and the entry in the register is made only after payment (SHA~\S~\ref{shaen:wykonanie}).
  \item After an Ordinary Departure a 12-month non-compete applies; a 12-month prohibition on actively soliciting employees and clients applies on every departure (SHA~\S~\ref{shaen:konkurencja}).
\end{itemize}

% ================================================================
\begin{Komentarz}
A buy-back of Unreleased Shares at the issue price is a market-standard solution, but a court may examine its compliance with the principles of community life (Articles~58 and 353$^{1}$ of the Civil Code (KC) \Zr{kc}); for this reason the agreement justifies the price by a mechanism accepted by all Founders when the Company was formed (SHA~\S~\ref{shaen:wykonanie}(2)). For a transfer below market value, the personal income tax consequences must be assessed \Zr{pit}. The Board should determine the cessation of involvement within 30 days --- a delay does not remove the transfer obligation, but weakens the Company's position in any dispute.
\end{Komentarz}

% ================================================================
\begin{Komentarz}
Under Article~210 KSH it is generally accepted that an agreement concluded with a member of a corporate body in breach of the special rules of representation is invalid (\Zr{sn4218}, \Zr{jara}); the same risk applies under Article~300$^{79}$ KSH. The safest course is to adopt, upon formation, a single shareholders' resolution appointing an attorney for the IP agreements and implementing agreements with directors (Art.~\S~\ref{en-art:final}(6)) and to ensure that the attorney is not a party to the given agreement.
\end{Komentarz}
